An inbox that never asks for a refresh

The hero is the inbox as a working mockup. Below is what keeps it live: one hub connection that drops, retries and rejoins on its own, and a widget that holds a customer’s messages until the network is back.

  • 01

    Tabs by ownership

    New and unassigned, assigned to me, all assigned, resolved, archived and irrelevant. Who sees all assigned depends on the role’s access level.

  • 02

    Assign to a team or a person

    An agent hands a conversation to a group and, optionally, one agent. The hub tells every open inbox, so it moves tabs everywhere at once.

  • 03

    Custom views

    Saved filters by channel, tag, group, agent and date become tabs of their own, and live events only reach the views they match.

  • 04

    Typing and receipts

    A typing event lights its conversation in the list for two and a half seconds; read receipts, tags and notes travel over the same connection.

From sign-up to the first chat

The tenant portal turns a sign-up into a workspace with a plan. Then the agent portal takes over, and the two pass the session back and forth so nobody signs in twice.

  • 01

    Two tokens, one interceptor

    Chat calls carry the portal’s token; plans, wallet and AI calls carry the tenant’s. One interceptor picks the right one and the right base URL.

  • 02

    Plans gate features

    Widget limits, WhatsApp and Messenger, the AI bot and voice features follow the plan. At the widget limit, Create is disabled and an upgrade prompt takes its place.

  • 03

    A wallet for voice

    Voice to voice and voice streaming draw from a prepaid wallet, each with its own switch and its own usage line.

  • 04

    Back to the right screen

    The agent portal opens the tenant portal on subscriptions, users, profile or security, carrying the session with it.

Decisions and what I’d change

Five repos, one hub, and a widget that has to behave on websites nobody on the team has ever seen.

  1. 01

    An iframe and a small loader

    The chat is an Angular app inside an iframe, so a store’s CSS can’t break it and its styles can’t leak out. The loader is about 400 lines of plain JS and CSS: launcher, iframe, back button closes the chat.

  2. 02

    The microphone lives on the host page

    Many sites never grant a microphone to a third-party iframe. Voice notes are recorded by the loader on the host page and posted into the chat as data, with start, stop and cancel as messages.

  3. 03

    Nothing a customer types is lost

    The widget keeps the conversation in IndexedDB, queues messages while offline and sends them once the hub is back. A message that still fails gets a resend button.

  4. 04

    Splitting the widget’s hub service

    One service had grown to own the connection, the listeners, sending and the visitor’s identity. In 2026 I split it into four, and four copies of the message template into components.

  5. 05

    Register hub listeners once

    The portal registers its 22 listeners again after every reconnect, and SignalR keeps both copies. Today I’d register them once, rejoin on reconnect, and use one typed event map instead of 22 untyped subjects.

  6. 06

    Keep tokens out of URLs

    The two portals hand a session over in a query string. Today I’d pass a one-time code and swap it for a session on the server, so a token never sits in history or logs.
