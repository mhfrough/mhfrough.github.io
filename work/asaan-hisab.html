Asaan Hisab.Bookkeeping, in plain words.

Asaan Hisab means easy accounts. It is free accounting and invoicing for small businesses in Pakistan: the owner records a sale or a bill in plain words, and the app keeps proper double-entry books underneath. It was my first production project, a month after graduating.

Decisions and what I’d change

A first job, an accounting product, and two developers doing most of the building.

  1. 01

    Words in front, ledger behind

    “Add income” and “Add expense” are what an owner understands. The same screen keeps a journal entry for the accountant, so nobody needs a second tool.

  2. 02

    Split instead of retyping

    One bank deposit often pays for two things. Splitting it into categories kept the record true to the bank statement, which is what people check it against.

  3. 03

    Reports read the ledger, not the forms

    Every report is worked out from posted lines, so profit and loss, the trial balance and the balance sheet can’t disagree with each other.

  4. 04

    Paper that looks official

    Clients registered for sales tax expect an invoice with the tax on every line, both sides’ NTN and the total in words. The government-standard layout prints exactly that, straight from the invoice.

  5. 05

    Desktop first

    Bookkeeping happens at a desk, and in May 2020 I took the phone layout out of the app shell; only the sign-up page stayed responsive. Today I’d build the app responsive from the first screen.

  6. 06

    Typed forms, one money type

    Amounts went through masks, pipes and string fixes in several places. Today: typed reactive forms and one money value object, so rounding and tax live in exactly one function.
