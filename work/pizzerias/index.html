The rules on the menu

Pizza ordering looks simple until the prices depend on the size, the extras depend on the size too, and delivery depends on where you live.

  1. 01

    Sizes

    Each pizza has a price per size. Extras like more cheese have one too, so the add-on follows the base.

  2. 02

    Deals

    A deal is a fixed price for a set of slots: pick one pizza from this category, one drink from that one.

  3. 03

    Areas

    Delivery areas each carry a fee and a minimum order. Pickup has no minimum.

  4. 04

    Vouchers

    A fixed amount or a percentage off, each with its own minimum order, checked by the API.

Checkout and the account behind it

A phone number is the account. Everything else follows from it: sign-in, codes, orders and notifications.

  • OTP sign-up

    Register with a phone number and confirm it with a one-time code, with a resend countdown. Resetting a password works the same way.

  • Cash, card or PayPal

    Cash on delivery everywhere. Card and PayPal per brand, switched on in the settings. PayPal returns to a page that verifies the payment.

  • Push notifications

    Firebase Cloud Messaging on the web, one project per brand, so each restaurant notifies its own customers.

  • Orders

    Active, completed and cancelled orders in tabs, each with the full breakdown: items, extras, tax, delivery and discount.

  • Closed is a state

    When the kitchen is closed the cart says so and checkout stays shut, instead of taking an order nobody will cook.

  • Back office

    A role-based portal where staff run orders, staff and products across all the brands.

Decisions and what I’d change

Small restaurants, a small budget and a new brand every few months.

  1. 01

    Fork per brand

    A fork made each new restaurant a matter of days, and no brand’s redesign could break another. The cost: a fix had to be copied eight times. Today I’d keep one codebase and load the brand at runtime.

  2. 02

    Areas, not addresses

    Customers pick a delivery area instead of typing an address for geocoding. No maps bill, and the kitchen decides how far it delivers and what each area costs.

  3. 03

    Polling over sockets

    An order changes state four or five times in an hour. A check every 10 seconds plus a push notification felt live enough, and needed nothing new on the server.

  4. 04

    Prerendered menus

    The first generation prerendered its pages with Scully, so they arrived as HTML, ready for search engines and slow phones. Most later forks dropped it for simpler builds.

  5. 05

    Card fields belong to the provider

    Card details went to our API, which charged them. Today I’d use the payment provider’s hosted fields, so card numbers never pass through our servers at all.

  6. 06

    Translations from day one

    Only Joker Pizza and Cloud Kitchen switch between German and English. Copy hard-coded in the other forks made that a retrofit; strings in files from the start would have cost nothing.
