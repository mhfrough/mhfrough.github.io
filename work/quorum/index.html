One answer hides the tradeoff

Ask a single assistant about a hard call and you get one balanced voice: some pros, some cons, a careful "it depends". The real tension behind the decision stays buried, and you still have to dig it out yourself.

Quorum splits that one voice into seven. Each member decides with its own method, on its own model, and none of them can see the others. Disagreement is the point: the report names each clash, so you see the tradeoff instead of a blend.

quorum

noun

The minimum number of members a group needs before its decision counts.

How a council runs

One command. The skill handles the fan-out, keeps every member in character, and hands back a single report.

  1. Frame

    The question becomes a clear decision: the options, what is known, what is not, and whether it can be undone.

  2. Fan out

    Up to seven separate sub-agents start in parallel, each on its own model. None of them can see the others.

  3. Vote

    Every member votes up or down in its own voice, and names the one fact that would flip its vote.

  4. Count

    Up is +1, down is -1. Above zero is YES, below is NO, and a tie breaks toward the more reversible option.

  5. Ask

    The Quorum only advises. Nothing is edited, sent or changed until you say yes.

A product site with one rule set

The Quorum site is one HTML page with no framework and no CDN. Its look comes from three rules, applied everywhere, including this page.

Shape

Anything you can press is a full pill. Cards and panels use a 20px radius, and blocks inside a card use 14px.

Warm off-black and warm ink, with one coffee accent and nothing else saturated. Light and dark themes share every token.

Then they got a board

Under the hero, all seven wander a low-poly chess board in three.js. Hover to wave, click to chat, ask a question and all seven answer at once.

  • Each member moves like a chess piece that fits it, and talks like itself.
  • Poke one three times and it gets annoyed, five times and it snaps, steams and storms off.
  • three.js r160 is self-hosted and fetched about two screens early. A skeleton drawn to the board’s shape holds the space, so nothing shifts when it fades in.
  • Without WebGL the council still talks, as a plain list of speech bubbles.

Both reels are animated HTML pages rendered to video (the board reel drives the real board code), with a recorded voiceover and English captions.

Built to pass its own tests

A Playwright suite runs on a desktop and a phone profile and checks the content against the skill, the interactions, the layout and the SEO.

  • Self-hosted

    Fonts, icons and three.js live in the repo. A test fails if the page requests anything off-site except the GitHub star count.

  • Search and sharing

    Canonical, Open Graph and Twitter tags with a 1200x630 card, JSON-LD for the app, author, site and both videos, plus robots.txt and a sitemap.

  • Accessible

    Captions on both reels, a keyboard-driven question picker, pressed states on toggles, the faded disclaimer checked at 4.5:1 in both themes, and all content shown with reduced motion.

  • Lean

    WebP posters load lazily, nothing big downloads until play, and looping shimmers and the marquee pause off screen.
