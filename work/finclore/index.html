The public site

The offer, the seven steps, services with prices and timelines, and add-ons. Prerendered for search, installable as an app, and set in the logo’s navy and red.

The original build rendered against a sample API: the live domain is gone. Services, timelines and the notice bar are samples.

The mark

I designed the logo: a wordmark ending in a full stop, and an F. monogram for every square the brand had to fill. Two colours, the US flag’s own navy and red, for a company that forms US companies.

Letter
Jost Bold, the geometric face the whole site is set in, tracked in so the word reads as one shape.
Full stop
Red, enlarged, and sitting on the baseline. It starts exactly where the top arm of the F ends.
Weights
A 16 unit stem, a 14 unit top arm and a 12 unit middle arm, thinner so the F does not look top heavy.
Colours
Navy #0A3161 and red #B31942, the official blue and red of the US flag.

The life of an order

A customer sees five states on every company they start. Staff work the last three as queues, one set for each service.

  1. 1Info provided
  2. 2Payment
  3. 3New
  4. 4Pending
  5. 5Complete
  • 01

    Saved per step

    Every screen posts to the API and the order remembers the last one, so the wizard reopens where the customer stopped.

  • 02

    Priced per state

    Each line is the service price plus the chosen state’s filing fee, summed in the order summary beside every step.

  • 03

    Locked at checkout

    Once an order reaches payment, its registration turns read only. Agent, EIN, logo and website add-ons lock on their own.

  • 04

    Documents back

    A completed order carries its filed documents, downloaded from the customer’s list of companies.

Decisions and what I’d change

A long form, money at the end of it, and customers in other time zones who rarely finish in one sitting.

  1. 01

    Save every step

    The first screen asks only for a name and an email and creates the account. From then on each step is saved on the server, so a founder can stop at the members step and come back a week later.

  2. 02

    Permissions as endpoint names

    Roles are lists of the API’s own endpoints, like GET::assign. The back office checks the same strings, so the screen and the server can never disagree about who may see a queue.

  3. 03

    One app, three audiences

    The public site, the portal and the back office share one project, one facade and one design. Lazy modules and separate guards keep a visitor from downloading any of the staff screens.

  4. 04

    A logo that survives a favicon

    The wordmark only works wide. The F. monogram was drawn for the small squares: favicon, install icon and avatar, with the full stop kept red down to 16 px.

  5. 05

    Payments behind one interface

    The payment provider changed in 2024 and the switch meant editing the payment screen itself. Today I’d put every provider behind one interface, so the next change is a new class, not a new page.

  6. 06

    Typed forms for a long wizard

    The wizard runs on template-driven forms and loosely typed models. Today I’d use typed forms with one model per step, so a renamed field breaks the build instead of an order.
