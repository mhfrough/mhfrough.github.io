Case studyAug 2023 to Feb 2024, loan-management platform

Thardeep Microfinance Foundation.Nine desks between a loan and its cheque.

TMF runs every loan through one staff web app, its loan-management platform: borrowers and their groups, a quote, a field visit, an approval chain from branch to CEO, then a cheque and a repayment schedule. In a small team I built its settings, the calculator and the borrower screens, worked on the approval desks and moved the app from Angular 11 to 15.

The life of a loan

Six stages, and no desk decides without a written comment. Loans go to individuals or to groups, and a group needs its members and guarantors inside the policy’s limits before it can apply.

  1. 1Borrower registered
  2. 2Loan quoted and applied
  3. 3Verified in the field
  4. 4Approved up the chain
  5. 5Disbursed by cheque
  6. 6Repaid in instalments
  • 01

    Who

    Identity card and its expiry, education, profession, income and source, with guarantors tied to the borrower.

  • 02

    Group

    Members and guarantors counted against the group limit policy before a group loan can go ahead.

  • 03

    Terms

    A product and its tenure band set the amount range, the markup rate, the due-date window and the fees.

  • 04

    After

    A schedule with markup on the falling balance, a penalty fee for late days and a provision book by portfolio age.

Decisions and what I’d change

An app a team had grown for two years, TMF’s lending rules in flux, and six months to make the money side dependable.

  1. 01

    Limits are data

    Who may approve how much changes with every policy meeting. Keeping each role’s products and ceilings in settings meant a new limit was a form, not a build.

  2. 02

    Permissions named after calls

    Every key is the HTTP method and path it guards, down to each desk’s approve, hold and reject buttons. The menu hides exactly what the API would refuse, and a new screen needs no new naming scheme.

  3. 03

    Officers see their products

    The calculator lists every product for an admin and only the assigned ones for everyone else, so a quote never starts from a product the officer cannot sell.

  4. 04

    Upgrade before features

    The app was on Angular 11. I took it through 13 to 15 in two days, deleting dead modules, unused styles and mock files on the way, so the next features were built on a supported version.

  5. 05

    One desk, not nine

    Each desk is its own copy of the same view with different buttons. Today I’d build one desk component driven by a config of stage, next desk and allowed actions, so a tenth desk is a line, not a folder.

  6. 06

    Types on the money

    Much of the code passes loans around as untyped objects. I’d add typed models and typed reactive forms for amounts, tenures and schedules, where a wrong field costs real money.
