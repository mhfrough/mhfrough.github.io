Malik Transport Solutions.A price before the call, a driver after it.

A courier and transport company in Rüdesheim am Rhein, Germany. I built its web platform in Angular: a public site where anyone prices a job by vehicle and address, and one portal where customers book, the office accepts and assigns a driver, and drivers run the job, log their shifts and get their payslips.

The public site

Home, company, price calculator and tariffs, plus six policy pages, in the company’s blue. The calculator searches real addresses as you type and returns distance, time and fare for the chosen vehicle.

The original production build rendered against a sample API. Addresses, fares and tariffs are samples; the home slider and the contact block are left out.

One API, three doors

Visitors price a job without an account. Everyone else signs in to the same portal, and their role decides what it shows: one customer’s orders, the whole office, or one driver’s day.

The stack

Decisions and what I’d change

A small company, one developer, and a portal that had to serve customers, the office and drivers from the first release.

  1. 01

    Addresses through the API

    The page never loads Google Maps. The address box waits for four characters and a 300 ms pause, asks the company API for suggestions, and a second call turns the chosen one into coordinates. A list of places did not need a map script.

  2. 02

    One portal, three roles

    Customers, office and drivers share one lazy-loaded portal. After sign-in the role picks the address, /portal for customers and /admin for staff, and the menu and every list show only that role’s items and actions.

  3. 03

    The fare lives in one place

    Each vehicle has a plan: a price per half hour with a ceiling, a price per load kilometre and a waiting charge. The office edits it in the portal; the calculator and new orders read the same numbers from the API, and the order keeps the fare it was booked at.

  4. 04

    Print, not a PDF library

    The first delivery note drew HTML to a canvas and saved it with jsPDF. I switched to a plain HTML page the browser prints: sharp text, the server’s QR code as it is, and a PDF whenever the office picks Save as PDF.

  5. 05

    Guards, not only a hidden menu

    Roles hide menu items and lists, and the API checks the token. Today I’d add a route guard per role as well, so a typed address never opens a screen that role should not see.

  6. 06

    One list, not six

    Each order list is its own component with the same table and paging, and each one fetched every customer to show their names. Today it would be one list with a status input, and names joined on the server.
